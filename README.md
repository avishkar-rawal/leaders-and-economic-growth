# Do Leaders Matter for Economic Growth?

An empirical research project examining whether political leaders' backgrounds influence economic growth, and whether institutional constraints amplify these effects.

## Research Questions

1. Do individual leaders matter for economic growth?
2. Do leaders' educational and military backgrounds affect economic performance?
3. Does an institutional void amplify the effects of leaders' backgrounds?

## Methodology

- **Data:** LEAD, Archigos, Polity5 and World Bank WDI.
- **Sample:** 155 countries, 1962–2018.
- **Methods:** OLS regression, country and year fixed effects, interaction terms, placebo tests and robustness checks.
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the country level.

## Key Findings

- Graduate education and military service show no statistically significant relationship with economic growth.
- Former rebel leaders are associated with approximately 0.85 percentage points lower annual GDP growth.
- The negative association is stronger when former rebels seize power and in countries with weak executive constraints.
